The Lighthouse Keeper's Notebook

An original short story for English learners.

Mira lived in a small town by the sea. Every morning she walked along the beach and collected shells. She liked the quiet sound of the waves and the smell of salt in the air.

At the end of the beach stood an old lighthouse. Nobody had lived there for many years. The white paint was peeling, and the windows were covered with dust. People in the town said it was abandoned, but Mira was curious.

One cloudy afternoon, she noticed that the door of the lighthouse was open. She hesitated for a moment. Then she took a deep breath and stepped inside.

The room was dark and cold. A narrow staircase climbed up the wall in a long spiral. On a wooden table near the door, Mira found a small leather notebook. Its pages were yellow and fragile.

She opened it carefully. The first page said: "To whoever finds this book: keep the light alive."

Mira read every page. The notebook belonged to the last lighthouse keeper, an old man named Thomas. He had written about storms, ships, and the brave sailors who depended on his light. He described how he climbed the stairs every evening to light the great lamp, even when he was tired or sick.

When Mira reached the last page, the sky outside had grown dark. Rain began to fall, and the wind became stronger. Far away on the water, she saw a tiny light. It was a fishing boat, and it was struggling against the storm.

Mira did not wait. She ran up the spiral staircase, one step after another, until she reached the top. There, she found the old lamp and a box of matches. Her hands were trembling, but she managed to light it.

A bright beam swept across the dark sea. Slowly, the little boat turned toward the harbor and reached the shore safely.

The next morning, the whole town talked about the mysterious light. Mira smiled and said nothing. She kept the notebook in her bag, and every evening after that, she climbed the stairs and kept the light alive.
